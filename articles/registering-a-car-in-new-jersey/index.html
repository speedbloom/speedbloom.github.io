Paperwork

Registering a car in New Jersey

Nothing about this is hard. It is simply unforgiving of being done out of order — one missing signature and you make the trip twice. Here is the sequence for a car bought from a private seller, with what needs to be in your hand at each stage.

Drive Thru Deals · September 17, 2026 · 5 min read

First, which version of this applies to you.

You bought from a dealer

The dealership handles the title application and the registration as part of the sale, collects the tax and fees from you, and gives you plates or a temporary tag before you drive off. Your job is to read what you sign and keep your copies. This article is not really for you — though the last two stages still are.

You bought from a private seller

Every step below is yours. Nobody is checking your work and nobody is responsible for it. That is the whole reason a private-party car is usually cheaper, and the reason the paperwork is the part that goes wrong.

1

Before any money moves, read the title

In daylight, with the seller present and the car in front of you. The VIN on the document has to match the VIN through the windshield and on the door jamb. The printed owner name has to match the person signing and the identification they are holding. The lienholder line has to be empty, or you need a plan for clearing it before you hand anything over. If any of those three is wrong, the rest of this list is irrelevant — see the title, explained.

Bring
  • Your phone, for photographs
  • Your own ID
  • A pen that works
2

Complete the assignment at the moment of sale

The seller signs the back of the title exactly as their name is printed on the front, dates it, and enters the odometer reading as it reads at that moment. Your name and address go in the buyer block, completely, in ink. Do not accept a signed title with your side left blank, and do not let anybody fill in a mileage figure that the car does not show. Write the bill of sale at the same table.

Bring
  • Bill of sale, two copies
  • Payment in a traceable form
  • The odometer reading, photographed
3

Insure the car before you drive it

New Jersey requires a registered vehicle to carry insurance, and the Motor Vehicle Commission will want evidence of it. Your insurer can usually add a vehicle by VIN over the phone in a few minutes, which is why the VIN photograph from stage one matters. Do this before you drive the car home, not after.

Bring
  • VIN
  • Your policy number
  • The insurance card or binder, printed
4

Work out the tax before you are standing at the counter

Sales tax on a private-party vehicle purchase in New Jersey is collected as part of the titling transaction, not paid to the seller. We are deliberately not printing a rate here, because rates and exemptions move and a stale number in an article is worse than no number at all. The New Jersey Division of Taxation publishes the current treatment, including how an unusually low purchase price is handled. Read it, work out your figure, and arrive knowing what you owe.

Bring
  • Purchase price, documented
  • Bill of sale
  • A payment method the agency accepts
5

Check what the counter needs, and whether you need an appointment

The MVC identifies applicants through a points-based documentation standard, and which of your documents count — and for how many points — is published on its site. Appointment requirements have changed more than once in recent years and vary by transaction type. Five minutes on the MVC's own pages the night before is the difference between one trip and three.

Bring
  • Identification that satisfies the MVC standard
  • Proof of NJ address
  • Appointment confirmation, if one is required
6

The transaction itself

Title application and registration application are handled together. The assigned title goes in, the tax and fees are paid, and the application is made in your name. If you financed the purchase, the lender is recorded as lienholder at this point and the title will reflect that when it is issued.

Bring
  • Assigned title
  • Insurance evidence
  • ID and proof of address
  • Payment
7

Plates, registration, and the title that arrives later

You generally leave with plates and a registration document. The title itself is issued afterward and mailed — to you if there is no loan, and handled according to the lienholder's arrangement if there is one. Put the registration in the car and the title somewhere that is not the car. A title in the glovebox is a title that leaves with the car if the car is ever stolen.

Bring
  • A screwdriver for the plates
  • Somewhere at home to file the title
8

Inspection

A newly registered car in New Jersey needs to be current on the state's inspection program. Deadlines, exemption periods for newer vehicles and the stations available to you all change, so confirm your due date against the MVC rather than against what a friend remembers. What the inspection does and does not examine is its own subject — we wrote that one separately.

Bring
  • Registration
  • Insurance card
  • Your inspection due date, confirmed

Where this actually goes wrong

Almost never at stage six. The counter is the place where errors are discovered, not the place where they are made. In practice the failures cluster in the first two stages, at the seller's kitchen table: a signature that does not match the printed name, a second owner on the title who was not there to sign, a lien nobody mentioned because the seller genuinely believed the loan was closed out, or a title from another state that carries a note nobody read. Every one of those is invisible until an MVC employee turns the document over, and by then you have already paid.

The fix is unglamorous and it is the same fix every time. Slow down at the table. Read the document. Photograph it before it is signed and after. If the seller is in a hurry at that specific moment, that is information.

General information, not legal advice. This is a plain description of a common sequence, not a legal opinion and not specific to your transaction. Required documents, identification standards, appointment rules, tax treatment, fees and deadlines are set by the State of New Jersey and change. Confirm every one of them with the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission, the New Jersey Division of Taxation, or the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs before you rely on it.

What we do with this

When a car leaves our lot, the title work is ours. We would rather do it than explain it, because an error we make is an error we have to fix, and six cars at a time does not leave room for repeat trips to an agency. But plenty of people who walk through here end up buying from a neighbor instead, and we would rather they did it properly than quickly. If you are mid-way through a private sale and something on the paperwork does not look right, come by. We will look at it with you. There is no version of that conversation where we sell you anything.

Written for New Jersey buyers in September 2026. No fee, tax rate, deadline or point value is quoted on this page on purpose — all of them change, and the authoritative versions live with the State. Verify with the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission and the New Jersey Division of Taxation.
