Buying

Where you buy changes what you pay

The same four-year-old crossover exists on a franchise lot, on an independent lot, in a driveway and on a website — at four different prices, with four different amounts of work already done to it and four different answers to "what happens if it breaks." Those four things are the same decision, not separate ones.

Drive Thru Deals · September 17, 2026 · 6 min read

We are one of the four channels below and have tried to describe the other three the way their own customers would. Each is the right answer for somebody, and the downsides of a small independent lot are named at the end along with everyone else's.

Every cell describes what is typical, not guaranteed. Sellers vary enormously within every column — a good independent lot beats a lazy franchise store on every row that matters.

The honest verdict on each

Franchise dealer Buy here when

You want the strongest possible safety net and you are willing to pay for it. A late-model car with factory warranty left, or a manufacturer-backed certified unit with a subvented finance rate, is a genuinely good product and no independent lot can replicate it — we cannot certify to a manufacturer's standard because we are not franchised to one. The trade-off is price and process: the highest numbers in the market, and the most structured back-end conversation.

Private party Buy here when

You are mechanically literate, or you have a mechanic you trust and the patience to use them on three cars before you buy one. The savings are the largest available anywhere. The trade-off is that every protection is removed at once — no Buyers Guide, no statutory warranty, no recourse, and you handle the title, the lien payoff and the MVC yourself. Buy the seller as much as the car. Our twelve questions for a private seller exist for exactly this.

Online retailer Buy here when

Your time is worth more than the margin, you dislike negotiating on principle, or nothing within driving distance fits what you want. A fixed price, a national inventory and a return window are real advantages, and the window is a consumer protection no driveway offers. The trade-off is that you bought a car you never drove, from a description — and fees, delivery charges and the financing rate can quietly reassemble the savings. Use the return window actively: a mechanic's inspection on day two is the whole point of it.

Independent lot Buy here when

You want a car that has been inspected and fixed by someone who has to see you in the grocery store, at a price between the franchise store and the driveway. That is the case for us, and it is a real one.

Now the downsides, which are ours. We hold six cars at a time, so if you want a specific trim in a specific color we probably do not have it and you will wait or go elsewhere. We cannot offer a manufacturer-certified car or a subsidized promotional rate — those exist only inside franchise agreements. Our warranty coverage is narrower than a brand's, and our service capacity is one shop, not a department with twelve bays, so a big repair takes longer with us than with a dealer who has loaner cars. And the quality of independent lots varies wildly: the category contains excellent operators and genuinely bad ones, and from the sidewalk they look similar. The published 168-point inspection and the price on the windshield are how we try to make the difference checkable rather than a matter of faith — but you should still check.

The one comparison that actually settles it

Whichever channel you pick, compare on the out-the-door price, itemized — vehicle, tax, title, registration, documentary fee, delivery — and on what the car will need in the next twelve months. Those two numbers are the only basis on which a franchise car, a private-party car and an online car can honestly be set side by side. A monthly payment was never designed to compare them.

Descriptions above generalize how each channel typically operates; they are not statements about any particular dealer, retailer or manufacturer's current programs, which change. Warranty, return and financing terms vary by seller and by vehicle — confirm them in writing before you buy.
