Buying

The first phone call

Six minutes, before you drive anywhere. Below is a call we wrote out in full — a constructed example, not a recording — with a note beside every line about what it is doing and why it sits where it does.

Drive Thru Deals · September 17, 2026 · 5 min read

The point of the first call is not to negotiate. It is to find out whether the car is worth an hour of driving, and to establish, early and pleasantly, that you are a buyer who checks things. Both of those get decided in the first two minutes, mostly by tone.

This transcript is constructed. Nobody said these words; we wrote both sides to make the annotation possible. The seller here is a reasonable one — not a villain — because the useful version of this exercise is the ordinary call, not the disaster.

Seller

Hello?

You

Hi — I'm calling about the silver crossover you have listed. Is it still available?

OpeningAsk the boring question first. It is the only one that makes the next five minutes worth having, and it gets a yes, which is a fine way to start.
Seller

It is, yeah. Had a couple people look at it but nobody's put anything down.

Free informationUnprompted, they have told you the car has been shown and not sold. Don't react to it. Note it and keep going — it will matter at the end.
You

Good. Can I get the VIN? I'd rather run it before I take up your afternoon.

The first real askThe VIN is the single highest-value thing you can get on this call, and asking for it early frames you as organized rather than suspicious. "Before I take up your afternoon" does the work — you are saving them time, not auditing them.
Seller

Sure. Give me one second, I'll text it to you off the registration.

Read the speedA seller who produces the VIN in under a minute has nothing filed away that they need to think about first. That is worth more than the VIN.
You

Appreciate it. How long have you had the car?

Establishing standingEverything they can tell you about the car is bounded by this answer. Three years of ownership means their description is testimony. Three weeks means it is a summary of someone else's testimony.
Seller

About four years. Bought it used with around thirty on it.

You

What's it needed in that time?

The pivotal questionNot "is anything wrong with it" — that invites a no. This one assumes, correctly, that a car four years in someone's hands has needed something, and makes the honest answer the easy one.
Seller

Tires last spring, brakes in front maybe a year before that. Battery. It had a sensor thing at one point that threw a light, the shop sorted it.

What a good answer sounds likeSpecific, ordinary, faintly dull. "A sensor thing" is vague but it is vague in the way real memory is vague. Ask for the invoice later, not now.
You

Do you have paperwork for any of that?

Low-stakes, high-yieldYou are not demanding records. You are finding out whether records exist. A folder in the glovebox changes what the rest of the call is worth.
Seller

Some of it. The tires for sure, that was at the place on the highway.

You

That's fine. Is there anything on it right now you'd want me to know about before I come out — a light, a noise, anything cosmetic?

The permission questionYou have just given them a socially easy moment to disclose. Most people take it. The ones who say "nothing at all" and then show you a car with a cracked bumper have told you how the rest of the transaction will go.
Seller

There's a scuff on the rear bumper, corner. And the rear wiper doesn't really park right, it stops halfway. Never bothered to fix it.

This is the best answer in the callTwo small unflattering specifics, volunteered. Specificity is expensive to fake. Nothing else they say today will be worth as much as this.
You

Good to know. Last thing — I'd want my own mechanic to put it on a lift before I buy. Are you okay with that?

Asked now, not laterThis is the question people save for the end and then don't ask. Raised on the phone it costs nothing; raised standing next to the car with the keys in your hand it feels like an accusation. The answer also sorts sellers faster than anything else here.
Seller

Yeah, no problem. I'd just want to be there.

Yes with a condition is a yesWanting to be present is normal. Refusing outright, or insisting on their own shop, is the answer that ends the conversation.
You

Works for me. I'll run the VIN tonight and call you tomorrow to set a time.

Close without an offerNo number has been discussed and none should be. You have not seen the car. Naming a price now either anchors you above what it's worth or insults a seller you may want to buy from.

— call ends, roughly six minutes —

What the call bought you

A VIN, an ownership span, a maintenance history in rough outline, two volunteered defects, the existence of at least some paperwork, and a yes on an independent inspection. That is enough to decide whether to spend an hour driving, which was the whole assignment.

It also bought a tone. You asked six questions, none of them adversarial, and the seller answered all of them without getting defensive. If any of that had gone differently — a VIN that took two days, "nothing wrong with it" said quickly, a no on the mechanic — you would have learned it for the price of a phone call rather than a Saturday.

Three things that did not happen

No price talk. Not one number. The listing price is a fact you already have; anything else is guessing about a car you have not touched.

No monthly payment. If the seller had been a dealer, a version of this call ends with them asking what you want to pay per month. The honest answer is that you are shopping for a car, not a payment, and you will talk about money once you have decided you want it.

No commitment. Nothing was held, nothing was promised, no deposit was discussed. A car that needs a deposit to survive until tomorrow afternoon is a car you were going to have to rush, and rushing is the only reliable way to buy the wrong one.

If you take one line from this page, take "What's it needed in that time?" It assumes wear instead of asking about faults, it is impossible to answer honestly with a flat denial, and the shape of the answer — specific and boring, or smooth and empty — tells you most of what a first call can tell you.

The conversation above is a composite written for this article. It is not a recording, a paraphrase of any real call, or a description of any real vehicle or seller. Nothing here states the policy of any lender, manufacturer or reporting service.
