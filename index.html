What we actually check

"168-point inspection" means nothing unless someone tells you what's on the list. Here's ours, in plain English.

Mechanical

  • Compression and leak-down
  • Cooling system pressure test
  • Transmission fluid condition
  • Timing belt or chain history
  • Every fluid, topped or replaced

Underneath

  • Frame and subframe corrosion
  • Suspension bushings and struts
  • Exhaust from manifold back
  • CV boots and axle play
  • Evidence of past collision repair

Brakes & tyres

  • Pad and rotor thickness, measured
  • Tyre tread depth at three points
  • Date codes — no dry-rotted spares
  • Parking brake adjustment
  • ABS and traction faults cleared

Inside & electrical

  • Every switch, motor and seat
  • Heat and air conditioning output
  • Battery load test, not a guess
  • All stored fault codes read
  • Both keys present and working

If something failed inspection and we fixed it, the invoice comes with the car. If something failed and we chose not to fix it — a stone chip, a worn interior trim — it's on the listing and reflected in the price.

How financing works here

One application goes to a panel of banks and credit unions. You see every offer that comes back — rate, term, monthly payment — on one sheet, the same sheet we're looking at.

  • 01Apply and we run it as a soft pull. Nothing hits your credit until you pick an offer.
  • 02We send it out and show you everything that comes back, including the offers we'd advise against taking.
  • 03You choose the term. We won't stretch a loan to 84 months to make the monthly number look smaller than the car is.
  • 04Out-the-door price in writing — vehicle, tax, title, registration, doc fee — before anything is signed.

Already leasing a car you like?

If your lease is ending and you'd rather keep the car than hand it back, that's not a sale off our lot — it's a buyout, and the numbers are worth checking well before your lease-end date.

We send that work to Champion Auto Finance, who arrange lease buyout financing for drivers nationwide.

Champion Auto Finance is not a lender; approvals and terms are subject to lender underwriting.

Sixteen years on the same corner

Speedbloom started in 2009 with four cars and a borrowed lift. The lot is bigger now, but not by much — and that's on purpose.

A small inventory means every car gets looked at properly. We buy six to eight vehicles a month, mostly lease returns and local trade-ins we know the history of, and each one goes through the shop before it goes on the line. If a car doesn't come out of inspection in a condition we'd put a family member in, it goes to auction rather than onto the lot.

Why the prices are posted

Every vehicle on this page has its price on it. There's no "call for pricing," no payment-first negotiation, and no four-square worksheet. You should be able to work out whether a car is worth your time before you spend an afternoon here.

We also post what's wrong with a car. A refinished bumper, a worn driver's seat bolster, high miles — those go in the listing. A used car with nothing wrong with it is either new or badly described.

On fees

There's no dealer prep fee, no window etch, no nitrogen-filled tyre charge and no reconditioning surcharge added at signing. Tax, title, registration and the state documentary fee are real and unavoidable, and we'll write all four down for you in advance. Anything beyond that, ask what it's for.

Trade-ins

We appraise on the spot and tell you the number we'd pay whether or not you buy something from us. If you'd do better selling it privately, we'll say so — we'd rather lose a trade than talk someone out of a few thousand dollars.

Questions we get a lot

Can I have the car inspected by my own mechanic?

Yes, and we'd encourage it. Take it to whoever you trust for a pre-purchase inspection. If your mechanic finds something ours missed, we want to know — that's a problem with our process, not with you.

Do you negotiate on price?

A little, but not much. Prices are set against what the car actually cost us and what comparable vehicles are selling for, not inflated to leave haggling room. If a car sits too long we'll reprice it publicly rather than wait for someone to ask.

What warranty comes with the car?

It depends on the vehicle. Newer cars often have factory powertrain coverage remaining, which transfers to you at no cost. Older ones are sold as-is under New Jersey's used car law, and we'll tell you plainly which is which before you buy.

Will you hold a car for me?

For a couple of days, yes, without a deposit. We'd rather hold a car for someone who's arranging financing than push them into a decision on the spot.

Why are you closed on Sundays?

New Jersey law prohibits the sale of motor vehicles on Sundays — it applies to every dealer in the state, not just us. If you find a car here on a Sunday, we'll hold it for Monday.

I'm leasing now. Can you help me buy out my lease?

Not directly — a lease buyout is a transaction with your leasing company, not a sale from our inventory. We refer that work to a financing partner who specialises in it. Worth starting a couple of months before your lease-end date, because payoff quotes expire.
